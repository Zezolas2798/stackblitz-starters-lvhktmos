## EX-6 — Consultancy Cardinality

### Stated professional evidence

- The founder has not observed one company hiring two consultancies to perform the same function at the same time.
- This does not exclude simultaneous specialist providers with distinct scopes; that case has not yet been tested directly.
- The last consultancy where the founder worked had approximately 20 active clients.
- Some clients had five operating units.
- One event-venue client had six event units plus a central unit where much of the food used by the other units was produced.
- The owner or manager normally contracted the consultancy or software.

### Implications

1. The one-active-consultancy rule remains plausible for the initial rollout but is not validated by one founder account.
2. The distinction between consultancy organization, individual consultant and specialist contractor must remain explicit in interviews.
3. Company structure cannot be modeled only as a flat unit count. Unit role and operational flows, such as central production supplying event units, are relevant to applicability and access scope.
4. Buyer, day-to-day user and company administrator may be different actors even when the owner or manager makes the purchasing decision.

### Remaining evidence obligation

Interview companies and consultants across the three initial segment groups, including multi-unit operations, and record provider count, provider scope, overlap period, unit coverage, buyer and contracting authority.
